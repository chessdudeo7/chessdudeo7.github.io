Opening

Who is sitting across the board

I’m a Computer Science student at the University of Waterloo, class of 2029, currently a software engineering intern at Ciena in Ottawa — where I’m porting an 1,800-line C optical-planning model to Rust and rebuilding its neural networks in HuggingFace Candle so the inference runs on hardware a decade older than the model.

Chess came first. I earned the Canadian National Master title, represented Team Canada at the 2019 World Cadet Championships, and spent a decade learning one habit that transfers cleanly to engineering: understand the position before you touch a piece. Most bugs, like most blunders, are a move played before the board was read.

These days that shows up as ML infrastructure and full-stack systems — computer vision pipelines, retrieval systems, and Rust/C interop. I’m open to co-op terms in 2027.

Things I’ve built, and what they cost to build

Computer vision 2026 —

ClipFarm

A full-stack pipeline that turns raw volleyball VODs into highlight clips. Ball tracking with RF-DETR detects rally contacts, refined by YOLOv8-pose classification. Serverless GPU inference with CPU fallback cut per-game tracking from 13 minutes to 8.7; a Cloudflare R2 cache keyed by video hash plus skip-decode frame sampling took another 31% off.

ML safety 2026

HUME

A structural firewall for an online belief-revision engine, built to block prompt injection. Confidence is revised through log-odds likelihood ratios drawn from structured provenance rather than from text, with structural OOD detection separating in-model contradictions from out-of-model regimes. Measured against a 19-probe adversarial battery.

Deep learning 2025

KnightMare

A chess engine that learns instead of searching. A PyTorch CNN trained on 100,000+ grandmaster games encodes each position as a 12-channel tensor and outputs a move distribution — no alpha-beta anywhere. Modal’s cloud GPUs turned hours of local training into minutes, which is the only reason it shipped inside a hackathon.
